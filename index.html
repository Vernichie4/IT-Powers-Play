IT in Entertainment

Behind every immersive world and engaging story are IT professionals who blend logic, design, and innovation.

Game Developer 🎮

Builds the systems and rules that make play possible — from player controls to AI and networking.

What they do — design and implement gameplay systems, physics, UI, and game logic. They translate design goals into performant code that runs across devices.

Core skills — programming (C#, C++, JavaScript), game engines (Unity, Unreal), debugging, data-driven design, and familiarity with math for movement and collisions.

Day-to-day — prototype mechanics, write and review code, optimize performance, integrate assets, and collaborate with artists, designers, and QA to iterate quickly.

How to get started — make small prototypes, follow engine tutorials, join game jams, read postmortems, and build a focused portfolio that highlights rule systems and polish.

Helpful tools — Unity; Unreal Engine; Git; VS Code or Visual Studio; performance profilers; simple physics libraries.

3D Artist 🧑‍🎨

Creates the characters, environments, and visual polish that give games personality and atmosphere.

What they do — model, texture, rig, and light scenes and characters. They move concepts from 2D sketches into optimized 3D assets ready for real-time use.

Core skills — 3D modeling, UV unwrapping, texturing, sculpting, basic rigging, shading, and an eye for composition and color.

Day-to-day — iterate on concept art, sculpt or model, bake textures, import into engine, fix shading and LOD issues, and work with developers to ensure assets run smoothly in-game.

How to get started — practice with Blender or other DCC tools, study anatomy and lighting, follow asset pipelines, build a portfolio of game-ready models, and participate in asset packs or collaborations.

Helpful tools — Blender; Substance Painter; ZBrush or Sculptris; Photoshop; engine import pipelines for Unity/Unreal; texture baker tools.

How IT Transformed Play

1972

Pong launches the video game revolution, introducing simple yet addictive digital entertainment. This marks the beginning of IT's crucial role in gaming.

1990s

The rise of the internet revolutionizes gaming through LAN parties and early online multiplayer. Games like DOOM and Quake showcase networking's potential in entertainment.

2007

The iPhone debuts, transforming phones into gaming devices. Mobile development explodes, making games accessible to billions and creating new career opportunities.

2020s

AI and Virtual Reality reshape gaming experiences. Machine learning powers NPCs, procedural generation creates infinite worlds, and VR delivers unprecedented immersion.
